The Protocol

Three pillars of device trust

Pillar 1

Provenance

Every device gets a permanent digital identity linked to its UDI and validated against FDA and EU regulatory databases. Every ownership transfer and service event is recorded as a credentialed, time-stamped entry. No handoff overwrites the history. No actor can edit what another recorded.

Pillar 2

Service History

Every maintenance visit, calibration, software update, and repair is recorded as a verified, time-stamped entry - signed by the credentialed actor who performed it, regardless of employer. The service history travels with the device. When a buyer or auditor asks, the record is a query - not a multi-day assembly exercise.

Pillar 3

Compliance

Audit evidence is built continuously, not assembled under pressure. Credentialed lifecycle events recorded across every organisational boundary become a verifiable compliance evidence record. For a refurbisher: a verified pricing signal. For a manufacturer: continuous regulatory readiness. For a buyer or insurer: independently verifiable proof.

The Scope

What MedPassport is

MedPassport is a neutral evidence layer. It preserves signed, independently verifiable proof of device lifecycle events across the boundaries where today's systems lose the record. It works alongside your existing quality, service, and regulatory systems, not in place of them.

Two ways to record evidence. Connect MedPassport to an existing CMMS, or use the MedPassport app with a barcode scan in the field. Either path produces the same verified record.

The Information Gap

Four structural failures in every device lifecycle

FSCA evidence that cannot be independently verified

MDR Art. 87 requires manufacturers to maintain evidence of corrective action across every device in their chain - regardless of current owner or service provider. Without a neutral evidence layer, that evidence depends on voluntary cooperation from every prior actor. A high-profile Class I FSCA in the respiratory device category demonstrated this at institutional scale. Hospitals and rental-fleet operators managing affected devices could locate their units, but could not produce tamper-evident, independently verifiable proof of corrective action at serial-number level across multiple service parties for regulatory submission. The limitation was not operational. It was structural: no neutral record existed that all parties could independently reference. Read moreShow less

EU MDR 2017/745 Art. 87 - FDA Class I FSCA public record

Unverified history reduces resale confidence

Buyers cannot independently price the liability of trusting a seller's paper records across an ownership boundary. The result is a material, structural discount on every device whose history cannot be verified by a neutral party. In 2026, regulators, buyers, and procurement teams are scrutinising refurbishment documentation and device traceability more closely than at any prior point. Compliance has become a commercial differentiator at point of sale, not only a regulatory requirement. Verified history removes the information asymmetry that makes the discount structurally unavoidable, converting a liability the buyer cannot price into a record they can independently inspect. Read moreShow less

Industry characterisation - ReLink Medical, March 2026

Audit evidence assembled under pressure

When a notified body arrives, evidence lives across ERP, CMMS, QMS, email threads, and paper binders, in systems owned by different organisations that do not talk to each other. The gap is structural: it exists between systems, not within them. For devices serviced by an independent ISO, the hospital cannot produce the ISO's service record without contacting that ISO directly and waiting for their response. The OEM's service log, the calibration binder, and the ISO's work order are each complete within their own system. None transfers automatically when a device crosses an organisational boundary. The multi-day audit preparation exercise is a symptom of that structural gap, not a process failure that better internal tooling can fix. Read moreShow less

Structural characterisation - validated by clinical engineering practice

Liability that rests on self-reported evidence

When a warranty claim or liability investigation opens, both parties reconstruct history from their own records. Documentation gaps feature in liability proceedings and influence underwriting terms. MedPassport creates the shared, independently anchored record both parties can reference. When an adverse event, warranty dispute, or post-market investigation occurs, every party involved reconstructs their version of device history from their own records. The OEM's service log, the hospital's CMMS, the ISO's paper report, and the refurbisher's handover certificate are all self-reported, none independently verified, none held by a neutral party. MedPassport does not eliminate liability or guarantee legal outcomes. What it creates is a shared, independently anchored evidence chain, so when an investigation begins, both parties are looking at the same record, not reconstructing competing ones. Who It Serves

One protocol. Four audiences.

The Manufacturer

The PMS obligation follows the device, not the contract. MedPassport provides the missing device-level evidence layer across every boundary the device crosses after it leaves your visibility. When a device has been serviced by an independent ISO, refurbished by a third party, or operated past the OEM cloud contract, the manufacturer cannot access that service evidence without voluntary cooperation from every actor in the chain. MedPassport provides the missing evidence layer, not by taking on service responsibility, but by making every credentialed service event independently accessible to the manufacturer's own PMS data collection process. The obligation ends only when the device reaches the end of its defined operational lifetime, a point that must itself be documented and provable. Read moreShow less

The Hospital

Service records from the OEM, an independent ISO, or any credentialed actor, in one place, role-governed, accessible without chasing the service provider. When a field safety notice arrives, clinical engineering needs to confirm every affected device immediately, including those with lapsed OEM contracts or independent service history. With MedPassport, that confirmation is a query against a single evidence chain, not a series of calls to each service provider. FSCA device identification takes seconds. When a notified body or regulator asks for audit evidence, the record is already assembled, not reconstructed under pressure across disconnected systems. Read moreShow less
