How MedPassport works

The evidence model, the two recording paths, and why no participant across the service chain ever interacts with blockchain infrastructure directly.

For every user in the chain

No blockchain complexity for your team

MedPassport is designed so that every user across the service chain never interacts with blockchain infrastructure directly. They log in, record the event, and the evidence record is created. The verification layer is on-chain and independently auditable. The user experience is a standard application, on desktop or mobile.

No crypto wallets. No tokens. No gas fees.

Participants authenticate with standard enterprise credentials. The backend infrastructure handles the chain interaction on their behalf. A technician logging a calibration event sees a form - not a transaction.

Independently verifiable, without exposing document content.

The verification layer is on-chain: anyone can confirm that a recorded event exists and has not been altered. The document content behind it stays in a role-gated vault. Any credentialed actor, or their appointed auditor, can verify integrity without relying on MedPassport as an intermediary, and without the underlying document ever becoming public.

Desktop and mobile. Connected and offline-tolerant.

Field technicians use a mobile progressive web application. Hospital and OEM teams use the desktop portal. Both produce the same credentialed, independently verifiable evidence record.

Works alongside existing CMMS and QMS systems.

MedPassport connects to existing service management systems via an adapter. Technicians do not maintain two sets of records. The CMMS stays the system of record for internal workflow. MedPassport is the cross-boundary evidence layer.

🚫
Patient data is excluded by architecture

Patient identity, clinical outcomes, and treatment records are not stored in MedPassport, not in the vault, not on-chain. This is an architectural constraint, not a policy. No actor can add patient data.

🔒
Each service organisation sees only its own events

A service technician can read the events they logged. They cannot read events logged by another ISO, the OEM, or the hospital - even on the same device.

🔁
Access follows the device, not the relationship

When a device changes owner, the previous owner's access closes. The new owner gains access. Access boundaries are enforced by the protocol, not by contract or trust.

The audit trail is built. The commercial data stays yours.
Service history, customer names, and pricing never leave your CMMS or ERP. MedPassport carries independently verifiable proof, not the underlying data.

Documents are stored in a role-gated vault using envelope encryption. Each document is encrypted with its own AES-256-GCM key. That key is then wrapped separately for every actor authorised to read it, so no single party, including MedPassport, holds a master key. Only a content hash and IPFS content identifier are anchored on-chain. The document itself never touches the public ledger.

Events are recorded to the ServiceLogRegistry contract on Polygon. Each entry is append-only, signed by the actor's delegated key via ERC-4337 account abstraction. No participant ever manages a private key or interacts with wallet infrastructure directly. The contract address, transaction hash, and block number are publicly verifiable by any party without requiring access to MedPassport.

The compliance score is computed by ComplianceScorer.sol and is publicly queryable at the smart contract layer. Score visibility in the application layer is controlled by requiring a Granted viewer authorisation before the score is exposed in any UI or API - protecting commercially sensitive scoring information while keeping the cryptographic verification layer fully open.

The full evidence model runs end to end on Polygon's public testnet: device identification against FDA and EU regulatory databases, credential verification, event recording, document anchoring through the encrypted vault, and compliance scoring. Every recorded event is independently verifiable on-chain by any party, without relying on MedPassport as an intermediary.

Regulatory bridges to FDA GUDID and EU EUDAMED are live and validated against the public registries. The architecture has been reviewed against ISO 13485 and GAMP 5 Category 4 risk classification for regulated business software.

Protocol design

Operator-free by architecture

No single organisation controls MedPassport. The protocol guarantees that no commercial relationship can prevent a credentialed actor from logging an event.

Axiom 1

No operator controls the protocol

MedPassport is MIT licensed. The smart contracts are publicly verifiable. No entity can be paid to exclude a competitor from recording a legitimate service event.

Axiom 6

Neutral between all commercial relationships

OEM and independent ISO events are recorded to the same evidence chain under the same rules. The protocol does not favour any party's account of device history.

Architecture

Dual-signature transfer governance

Ownership transfers require independent signatures from both the current owner and the incoming owner. Neither party can unilaterally assert a transfer occurred.
