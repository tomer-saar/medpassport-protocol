The evidence obligations MedPassport addresses

EU MDR, FDA QMSR, and EUDAMED each create specific evidence requirements that follow the device across its operational lifetime. This page sets out those requirements and how MedPassport addresses them.

On this page

  • EU MDR - Post-market surveillance
  • EU MDR - Field safety corrective action
  • EUDAMED - Registration and vigilance
  • FDA QMSR - Quality system regulation
  • What MedPassport does not replace
EU MDR 2017/745
In force - lifetime PMS obligation

Post-market surveillance

Article 83 of EU MDR 2017/745 requires every manufacturer to establish and maintain a post-market surveillance system for the full operational lifetime of the device. The obligation to collect and evaluate safety and performance data continues regardless of whether the service contract is active or whether the device has changed ownership.

The practical consequence is that a manufacturer's PMS responsibility does not end when the device leaves their contracted service network. When a device is serviced by an independent ISO, operated in a hospital outside the OEM service contract, or transferred to a new owner, the manufacturer still needs evidence of what happened to it.

Registration confirms a device exists. PMS evidence continues after registration, for the device's entire operational lifetime, across every owner and service provider. That is the gap MedPassport addresses.

MedPassport provides the cross-boundary evidence layer that makes this data accessible: every credentialed service event, regardless of which organisation performed it, is recorded to the same independently verifiable chain. The manufacturer's PMS data collection process can query that chain directly, without requiring voluntary cooperation from every prior service actor.

Source: EU MDR 2017/745, Article 83
EU MDR 2017/745
In force - notification and evidence obligation

Field safety corrective action

Article 87 requires manufacturers to notify the relevant competent authority of field safety corrective actions and to maintain evidence of correction across every device in their distribution chain - regardless of current owner, current service provider, or contract status.

In practice, this means locating and verifying every affected device, not just demonstrating that an FSCA programme was executed in aggregate. For high-risk devices, that increasingly means device-level evidence that corrective actions were communicated, performed, and traceable across the affected fleet.

When a device has crossed an ownership boundary or been serviced by an independent ISO, that evidence depends on voluntary cooperation from every prior actor, unless a neutral, independently verifiable record already exists. MedPassport provides that neutral evidence layer.

Source: EU MDR 2017/745, Article 87
EUDAMED
Mandatory - 28 May 2026

EUDAMED - Registration and vigilance

EUDAMED registration became mandatory on 28 May 2026. Registration confirms a device's existence in the EU regulatory system. It does not replace the post-market evidence obligations that continue under Articles 83 and 87.

28 May 2026
UDI and device registration mandatory

All Class IIb and Class III devices must be registered in EUDAMED. The registration obligation is now in force.

27 Nov 2026
Legacy device registration deadline

Devices placed on the market before 26 May 2021 must complete EUDAMED registration by this date.

~Q2 2027
EUDAMED Vigilance module - expected mandatory

When the Vigilance and post-market surveillance module becomes mandatory, PSUR submissions move to structured digital format through EUDAMED. The evidence MedPassport assembles today becomes a direct input to that submission. Note: EU regulatory timelines typically slip 6-12 months. This date is the current expectation, not a confirmed deadline.

Source: EU MDR 2017/745, EUDAMED published timeline, European Commission
FDA QMSR
In force - February 2026

FDA QMSR - Quality system regulation

The FDA Quality Management System Regulation (21 CFR Part 820, effective February 2026) incorporates ISO 13485 by reference. Because of this, the same evidence layer that supports EU MDR post-market surveillance also supports the ISO 13485 evidence requirements that QMSR now references in the US.

QMSR also incorporates ISO 13485 supplier controls (§7.4) and CAPA trend analysis (§820.100) by reference, both requiring service trend data across the full device population, including independently serviced devices. MedPassport supplies structured, queryable pass and fail data from every credentialed actor, OEM and ISO alike, as an input to that analysis. A single deployment built for EU evidence requirements extends naturally to support these US requirements as well.

QMSR §820.100 requires a corrective and preventive action process that includes data analysis from post-production sources. MedPassport provides independently verifiable service and complaint data from across the device chain - the cross-boundary evidence that CMMS and QMS systems generate internally but do not transfer between organisations.

MedPassport supplies data to the OEM's QMS and CAPA process. It does not replace either, and it does not itself determine regulatory compliance.

Source: FDA 21 CFR Part 820, effective February 2026 - ISO 13485:2016

What MedPassport does not replace

MedPassport is an evidence layer. It is not a quality management system, a CMMS, a clinical system, or a regulatory submission platform.

MedPassport does not replace: the manufacturer's QMS or CAPA process - the hospital's CMMS or internal service management - the OEM's service contract obligations - EUDAMED registration (a manufacturer obligation MedPassport does not participate in) - a notified body's conformity assessment - a regulatory submission

What MedPassport provides is the cross-boundary evidence layer that none of those systems can currently provide: independently verifiable proof of what happened to a device, across every owner and service provider, available to authorised parties at the moment they need it.
